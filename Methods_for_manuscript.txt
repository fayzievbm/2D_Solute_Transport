PROPOSED REPLACEMENT NUMERICAL-METHOD TEXT

Pressure integration and initial transient

The pressure field was expressed as q = p - p0, so that the downstream
condition becomes q = 0 while the prescribed inlet value is q = p1 - p0.
The physical pressure equation, permeability coefficients, storage
coefficient and boundary conditions were retained. A conservative spatial
discretization on the nonuniform mesh yields the linear initial-value
problem dq/dt = A q + b, with q(0) = 0. Here A contains both spatial
directions and b represents the finite inlet opening. This complete system
was integrated using an adaptive implicit backward-differentiation method
with orders 1-5 and the numerical-differentiation-formula enhancement
implemented in SciPy. The selected calculation used relative tolerance
1e-8 and absolute tolerance 1e-7 Pa for q.

Each implicit step solves the full coupled spatial system. A transverse
eigendecomposition, tridiagonal solves in the longitudinal direction and
a low-rank correction for the finite inlet accelerate this solve without
introducing directional time splitting. The structured solve was checked
against an independent sparse direct solve, and pressure time integration
was checked against a matrix-exponential reference on a smaller mesh.

The change in pressure integration addresses the spurious startup
overshoot observed with the previous directionally split pressure scheme
under the abruptly imposed inlet pressure. The boundary activation and
physical initial condition were retained; neither pressure clipping nor
an artificial ramp in the prescribed inlet pressure was introduced.
Pressure bounds were checked at every accepted adaptive state and at
every time requested by the transport calculation, allowing 1e-6 Pa for
roundoff. This is a verified property of the reported calculations, not
a general positivity guarantee for arbitrary BDF steps or parameters.

Transport integration and conservation

The conservative two-stage alternating-direction transport scheme and
the corrected adsorption/desorption increments were retained. Darcy
velocities were evaluated from the pressure field at the actual physical
midpoint for the longitudinal flux and at the beginning/end of each
transport step for the transverse flux. A smoothly increasing geometric
transport-step sequence covers the first 800 s, followed by a 2 s step
in the selected calculation. The step sequence is normalized to reach
800 s exactly, with no abrupt increase at the transition. The entire
sequence, including startup, was refined in the temporal study. The
time-dependent inlet concentrations were evaluated using physical time.

All four scenarios were recomputed: constant anisotropic dispersion and
variable scalar dispersion with constant inlet concentration, and constant
dispersion with exponential inlet histories having rate constants 0.001
and 0.0005 s^-1. Their physical parameters are listed in the accompanying
run summary and were not recalibrated during numerical verification.

The total discrete solute inventory was calculated as
M(t) = sum_ij V_ij [theta c_ij(t) + ca_ij(t) + cp_ij(t)].
The balance residual was evaluated from
R(t) = M(t) - M(0) - I(t) + O(t) + G(t),
where I, O and G denote cumulative boundary input, boundary output and
degradation, respectively. Boundary fluxes and degradation were taken
from the actual half-step updates. Accepted capacity-limited changes
in adsorbed concentration were used consistently in the dissolved-phase
source, avoiding double counting of adsorption or adsorbed-phase decay.
Every half-step contributed to the balance audit. No dissolved-phase
concentration clipping was used. Boundary nodes are algebraic values
and do not add storage; V_ij denotes the interior nodal control volumes.
The represented volume approaches the physical domain volume as the
mesh is refined. Integrated amounts retain the supplied concentration
basis per unit transverse thickness.

An independent hydraulic check compared Sp sum_ij V_ij q_ij(t) with
the time integral of the net Darcy boundary flux. The latter was
integrated over the pressure solver's piecewise-polynomial dense output
using three-point Gaussian quadrature, which integrates these scalar
polynomials exactly apart from floating-point and solution error.

Geometry and numerical verification

The inlet is a fixed 0.04 m opening at x=0, extending from y=0.38 to
0.42 m in the 0.8 m-high domain. Face-overlap fractions preserve this
width on every mesh. The remaining upstream wall and both transverse
walls are impermeable. The downstream condition is p=p0=100000 Pa;
the inlet pressure is p1=500000 Pa. The 64 m computational domain
represents a truncation of the semi-infinite domain. Its adequacy was
tested by extending it to 128 m. The plotted intervals (0-4 m or 0-2 m)
are distinct from the computational length.

Three nested meshes were obtained by bisecting every interval. The finest
mesh contains 1013 by 489 stored nodes, including boundary nodes; it is
nonuniform and must not be described using one uniform spatial step.
The complete coordinates accompany the numerical outputs. Field
comparisons use volume-weighted relative L2 differences over 0-4 m
at 0.5, 1, 2, 5 and 15 h, with q used for pressure normalization.
The finer or longer-domain solution is the comparison denominator.
Final inventories, cumulative inputs, cumulative degradation, selected
physical-point values and complete histories at (0.05,0.4) m were also
checked. The zero-reaction transport reference displayed approximately
second-order temporal error reduction. In the three-step full reactive
calculation, final inventory changes showed approximately first-order
temporal convergence; the complete coupled algorithm is therefore not
claimed to be globally second-order in time. The accompanying verification report gives the measured
changes and acceptance decision. Grid-to-grid differences and
conditional Richardson/GCI estimates are reported separately; neither
is presented as independent experimental validation.
